How it works

Listen. Identify. Explore.

A small listening station outside, your own Chirp server inside, and the app in your pocket.

01 · LISTEN

A station in the yard

A Raspberry Pi with one or more USB microphones listens all day. A short rolling buffer means the start of every call is kept, not just the end.

02 · IDENTIFY

BirdNET names the call

The Cornell Lab's BirdNET model identifies the species on the station, then your server checks it again with your location and the time of year.

03 · EXPLORE

Your yard, in your pocket

Today's birds, every recording with its spectrogram, a life list that builds itself, species profiles and alerts when someone new shows up.

Set up

Three pieces, one afternoon.

Chirp runs on hardware you own. Join someone's site with an invite code, or set up your own.

1 · Station

Put a listener outside

  • Raspberry Pi 5 with a USB microphone; add more microphones for a cleaner mix
  • Optional Google Coral accelerator for faster identification
  • A weatherproof spot within Wi-Fi range
  • Run the installer, point it at your server
Station setup guide →
2 · Server

Run Chirp at home

  • Docker on a home server, NAS or always-on computer
  • Stores recordings, runs the second BirdNET pass, sends alerts
  • Set your location for local species and eBird data
  • One server can host several sites, each with its own members
Server setup guide →
3 · App

Invite your household

  • Use the iPhone app, or open your server in any browser
  • Share an invite code so family and friends can join your site
  • Choose your alerts and the birds you watch
  • Live Detect works anywhere with your phone's microphone

Questions

Good to know

Where does my data go?

Recordings, identifications and accounts live on the Chirp server you or your site's owner runs. There are no ads, no tracking and no third-party sign-in. Species photos and facts come from public sources such as eBird, iNaturalist and Wikipedia.

How accurate is it?

BirdNET, from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Chemnitz University of Technology, recognizes thousands of species. Chirp checks every station detection again on the server with your location and the season, and you choose the confidence threshold that's shown.

Do I need my own station?

No. If someone has invited you to their site, open the app or ourchirp.com, tap Create account and enter their invite code. Live Detect also works on its own with your phone. See app setup.

How do I set up a station or server?

Follow the setup guide: the station, three ways to connect it (including automatic setup from your server), your own server, and the app. The user guide covers everyday use and settings.

Where is the code?

The station and server software are on GitHub ↗, with setup guides for the Raspberry Pi and the Docker server.

Does it cost anything?

The app is free, with no ads or purchases. A station needs a Raspberry Pi and a microphone.

Can I hear owls at night?

Yes. Chirp listens around the clock and has rules tuned on field recordings for owls and other night callers, so nighttime visitors show up without daytime noise being mistaken for them.
